# Lunar geometry data, provenance and validation

The final point supports intervals ending at the beginning of 2031; it does not provide a full 2031 prediction. SHA-256: `966bed14200d762ee124812b6de891c4c6d169e2a5101be303563750c0b4ce61`. This is a project-generated **calculated** table, not live telemetry or a NASA-provided table.

Results are not guaranteed accurate if a new leap second changes the mapping. Python input cannot represent `23:59:60`.

## Original 2026 fallback

`year_2026_ephemeris.csv` is the original 4,381-row two-hour, Moon-centred geometric Sun/Earth table covering 2026 through 2027-01-01. Its SHA-256 is `90c0d716388897af5c26ffc971d04e15817e5614ba92c4111447dca037c26ddd`. It was retrieved from a [third-party mirror](https://github.com/julianascencio/orbital-pv/tree/7c2b1a64f7d43b9662b022ac3b88351228bbd3cd/examples/lunar/_data) and matches that mirror's [provenance](https://github.com/julianascencio/orbital-pv/blob/7c2b1a64f7d43b9662b022ac3b88351228bbd3cd/examples/lunar/_data/provenance.json). The mirror reports NASA NAIF SPICE/DE421 calculation; its source kernel differs as noted above. The full NASA-origin chain was not independently verified here.

At runtime, the primary table is used when its checksum and structure pass. If it is unavailable or fails integrity, the verified original table is used **only for intervals fully covered by 2026**. Other intervals return unavailable rather than fabricated results. Loaded datasets are checked on first load and cached in process memory; restart to recheck files changed on disk. API and exports report which dataset and hash were actually used. Both tables are local after installation; internet is not required for geometry.

## Model and limits

Between two-hour grid records the app interpolates unit-vector direction, normalizes it, and linearly interpolates vector magnitude; it does not extrapolate. This avoids the large artificial range shrinkage produced by linear interpolation of raw rotating vectors, but **range and direction remain approximate**. UTC is converted during table generation with the pinned leap-second kernel, then vectors are interpolated between UTC labels at runtime.

The topocentric site is on a 1,737.4 km sphere plus user-selected height (0–1,000 m), with east-positive longitude. Azimuth is clockwise from local north; at a pole, the supplied longitude defines local axes. Azimuth is flagged undefined at zenith/nadir. A zero-degree threshold is the **local tangent plane**, even at nonzero observer height: the depression of a spherical horizon with height is not modeled. It omits terrain, slope, lunar shadows, finite Sun/Earth discs, light-time, aberration, Earth-antenna constraints, atmosphere and operations. Fixed clearances are not terrain.

Summary timeline totals and windows remain 15-, 30- or 60-minute **midpoint bins**. The separately labeled refined transitions scan elevation signs every 15 minutes and bisect detected sign changes until the bracket is at most one second. This is a numerical stopping tolerance, **not one-second physical accuracy**. Short events between scan samples, tangencies and multiple crossings may be missed. Refined overlap is the intersection of independently detected Sun/Earth windows; its totals can differ from midpoint-bin totals. At or near a pole, grazing cases can be especially sensitive to small model errors. The +2° threshold experiment is not a statistical uncertainty interval. Do not use this prototype for missions, safety, EVA, or communications decisions.

## Selected cross-checks — not an independent audit

`tests/cspice_reference.csv` contains 120 target cases: 10 UTC instants across 2020–2030 × six sites/heights × Sun/Earth. It is generated independently of the application's ENU code using CSPICE `azlcpo` on a spherical Moon (radius 1,737.4 km), `NONE` corrections and the **same kernels** as the primary table. Across these selected cases the observed largest differences after interpolation were approximately **0.00038° elevation, 0.00094° azimuth, 12.48 km Sun range and 1.00 km Earth range**. Eight selected direct-CSPICE threshold-crossing cases in `tests/cspice_crossings.csv` differed by at most about **17.11 seconds**. Scripts `generate_cspice_reference.py` and `generate_cspice_crossings.py` reproduce the fixtures with the pinned kernels. These are small, hand-selected regression cases, not random coverage, independent kernel verification, global error bounds, peer review or flight certification. The reference fixtures themselves use the same leap-second and lunar-orientation assumptions.

### Separate NASA/JPL Horizons DE441 vector cross-check

On 2026-11-14, five UTC instants were requested from NASA/JPL Horizons, once for [Sun](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/api/horizons.api?format=text&COMMAND=%2710%27&OBJ_DATA=%27NO%27&MAKE_EPHEM=%27YES%27&EPHEM_TYPE=%27VECTORS%27&CENTER=%27500%40301%27&TLIST=%272459030.546875%2C2460023.8125%2C2461359.041666667%2C2461831.03125%2C2462867.458333333%27&TLIST_TYPE=%27JD%27&REF_PLANE=%27FRAME%27&REF_SYSTEM=%27ICRF%27&OUT_UNITS=%27KM-S%27&VEC_CORR=%27NONE%27&TIME_TYPE=%27UT%27&CSV_FORMAT=%27YES%27) and once for [Earth](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/api/horizons.api?format=text&COMMAND=%27399%27&OBJ_DATA=%27NO%27&MAKE_EPHEM=%27YES%27&EPHEM_TYPE=%27VECTORS%27&CENTER=%27500%40301%27&TLIST=%272459030.546875%2C2460023.8125%2C2461359.041666667%2C2461831.03125%2C2462867.458333333%27&TLIST_TYPE=%27JD%27&REF_PLANE=%27FRAME%27&REF_SYSTEM=%27ICRF%27&OUT_UNITS=%27KM-S%27&VEC_CORR=%27NONE%27&TIME_TYPE=%27UT%27&CSV_FORMAT=%27YES%27). Request parameters: Moon center `500@301`, `VECTORS`, geometric `NONE`, ICRF, km, discrete JD UTC; Horizons identified its source as DE441. Selected X/Y/Z values were transcribed into `tests/horizons_de441_icrf.csv` (SHA-256 `894dfe9e54485d3bd07343cc9fe60b1d12f94cb88f6db3bee2b483bf7a29c471`); see the linked original responses for the full metadata and precision.

`scripts/generate_horizons_reference.py` rotates these Horizons ICRF vectors into the same `MOON_ME_DE421` orientation using the **same pinned frame/orientation/leap-second kernels** as the app, then uses CSPICE `recazl` to compute six site/height cases per instant. It approximates ICRF as J2000, so that frame distinction remains a limitation. The resulting 60 Sun/Earth target cases are in `tests/horizons_reference.csv`. For this selection, maximum differences were approximately **0.000379° elevation, 0.000943° azimuth, 11.94 km Sun range and 1.00 km Earth range**. DE441 versus DE421 is a separate ephemeris-source check, but **not an independent lunar-frame check**, scientific audit, or global error bound. Horizons data were retrieved via the web retrieval tool, not by the running app; the app remains offline-capable after installation.

### NASA/JPL Horizons high-precision lunar-orientation check

To avoid deriving the reference orientation with our own CSPICE kernels, we queried NASA/JPL Horizons on 2026-11-14 for **geometric** ICRF vectors from three lunar surface locations to the Moon center at the same five instants: [0°E, 0° latitude](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/api/horizons.api?format=text&COMMAND=%27301%27&OBJ_DATA=%27NO%27&MAKE_EPHEM=%27YES%27&EPHEM_TYPE=%27VECTORS%27&CENTER=%27coord%40301%27&SITE_COORD=%270%2C0%2C0%27&TLIST=%272459030.546875%2C2460023.8125%2C2461359.041666667%2C2461831.03125%2C2462867.458333333%27&TLIST_TYPE=%27JD%27&REF_PLANE=%27FRAME%27&REF_SYSTEM=%27ICRF%27&OUT_UNITS=%27KM-S%27&VEC_CORR=%27NONE%27&TIME_TYPE=%27UT%27&CSV_FORMAT=%27YES%27), [90°E, 0° latitude](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/api/horizons.api?format=text&COMMAND=%27301%27&OBJ_DATA=%27NO%27&MAKE_EPHEM=%27YES%27&EPHEM_TYPE=%27VECTORS%27&CENTER=%27coord%40301%27&SITE_COORD=%2790%2C0%2C0%27&TLIST=%272459030.546875%2C2460023.8125%2C2461359.041666667%2C2461831.03125%2C2462867.458333333%27&TLIST_TYPE=%27JD%27&REF_PLANE=%27FRAME%27&REF_SYSTEM=%27ICRF%27&OUT_UNITS=%27KM-S%27&VEC_CORR=%27NONE%27&TIME_TYPE=%27UT%27&CSV_FORMAT=%27YES%27), and [0°E, 90°N](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/api/horizons.api?format=text&COMMAND=%27301%27&OBJ_DATA=%27NO%27&MAKE_EPHEM=%27YES%27&EPHEM_TYPE=%27VECTORS%27&CENTER=%27coord%40301%27&SITE_COORD=%270%2C90%2C0%27&TLIST=%272459030.546875%2C2460023.8125%2C2461359.041666667%2C2461831.03125%2C2462867.458333333%27&TLIST_TYPE=%27JD%27&REF_PLANE=%27FRAME%27&REF_SYSTEM=%27ICRF%27&OUT_UNITS=%27KM-S%27&VEC_CORR=%27NONE%27&TIME_TYPE=%27UT%27&CSV_FORMAT=%27YES%27). Horizons labels the site orientation `MEAN_ME (high precision)`, the lunar radius 1737.4 km, and the ephemeris DE441. The transcribed `tests/horizons_lunar_axes.csv` (SHA-256 `f0060ff7fab81bd244e073de4cbe05e0ca869c31cdd45729b81aa1856f8da94f`) stores those site-to-center vectors; negating and dividing by the radius gives the lunar X/Y/Z axes in ICRF. The linked outputs retain the source metadata. Horizons and our model both use related JPL mean-Earth lunar orientation conventions, so this is a separate service/reference calculation, **not an independent physical measurement** or an unrelated model.

Using those axes and the previously archived Horizons DE441 Moon-center-to-target vectors, `scripts/generate_horizons_axes_reference.py` computes local ENU directly in ICRF with no CSPICE kernels or application code. It produces `tests/horizons_independent_axes_reference.csv` (SHA-256 `6ae88485d302c70996929232b7d728e65bf29bdc66a844caa8b9f7754ca748ec`). The fixture contains 60 selected cases, 12 within 2° of the tangent plane, across five instants and six sites/heights. Largest observed app-minus-reference differences: **0.000373° elevation, 0.001021° azimuth, 11.934 km Sun range, 1.000 km Earth range**. These include DE421/DE441, frame, table-interpolation, rounding and time-tag differences; they are selected-case observations, not global error bounds. Future UTC timestamps assume no unknown future leap seconds.

As an additional construction check, [Horizons geometric Earth vectors at 85°S, 0°E, 0 km](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/api/horizons.api?format=text&COMMAND=%27399%27&OBJ_DATA=%27NO%27&MAKE_EPHEM=%27YES%27&EPHEM_TYPE=%27VECTORS%27&CENTER=%27coord%40301%27&SITE_COORD=%270%2C-85%2C0%27&TLIST=%272459030.546875%2C2460023.8125%2C2461359.041666667%2C2461831.03125%2C2462867.458333333%27&TLIST_TYPE=%27JD%27&REF_PLANE=%27FRAME%27&REF_SYSTEM=%27ICRF%27&OUT_UNITS=%27KM-S%27&VEC_CORR=%27NONE%27&TIME_TYPE=%27UT%27&CSV_FORMAT=%27YES%27) are archived in `tests/horizons_earth_site_85s.csv`. The reconstructed ICRF vector components differ from those five direct outputs by at most 5.83e-11 km; this verifies the selected axis/sign/site-vector construction, not app accuracy at all coordinates. Tests reproduce the fixture and check each case. This external orientation benchmark addresses one previously identified validation gap, but is **not an independent expert audit**, a peer review, or proof of suitability for mission decisions. Broader cases, independent review and kernel source verification remain outstanding.
